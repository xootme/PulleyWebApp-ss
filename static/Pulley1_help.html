Belt Profile

Family
Selects the belt standard family. Each family defines the tooth shape and pitch range. Common families include:
  • HTD — High Torque Drive; rounded tooth profile, widely used in robotics and 3D printers.
  • GT / GT2 / GT3 — Gates Tooth profile; improved accuracy over HTD, common in precision motion.
  • T-series (T2.5, T5, T10) — Trapezoidal profile; older standard, lower backlash at slow speeds.
  • MXL / XL / L / H — Imperial trapezoidal series; common in legacy and US-market equipment.
Changing the Family resets the Pitch options to only those available for that family.
Pitch
The center-to-center distance between adjacent belt teeth, in millimetres (e.g. 5M = 5 mm pitch). A larger pitch means a coarser, higher-torque belt; a smaller pitch gives finer resolution and quieter operation.
Pitch is locked to Family — choose your Family first, then select the Pitch.

Size

Number of Teeth Input
The number of teeth on the pulley. Determines the pulley diameter directly — more teeth = larger pulley. A minimum tooth count is enforced per belt spec to prevent undercutting.
In Two Pulley Drive mode, Pulley 1 has the highest edit priority. Changing its teeth keeps the Gear Ratio fixed and recalculates Pulley 2 automatically.
Outer Diameter (mm) Auto
Calculated automatically from the tooth count and pitch. You can also type a diameter here — the tool finds the nearest whole-number tooth count and updates accordingly. In Two Pulley Drive mode, editing the OD here also recalculates Pulley 2 (same as editing teeth directly).
Bore Diameter (mm) Input
The diameter of the central shaft hole. Set this to match your shaft. The bore must be smaller than the pitch diameter. Minimum bore is 1 mm.
For 3D-printed pulleys, add 0.2–0.4 mm to account for printing tolerance.
Pitch Diameter Read-only
The theoretical diameter at which the belt pitch line runs. Used internally for belt length calculations. Pitch diameter = (Teeth × Pitch) / π.

Advanced

Tooth Clearance
Controls how much extra room exists at the tip of each belt tooth inside the pulley groove.
  • Tight — Minimal clearance; best for precision drives, harder to assemble.
  • Standard — Recommended default; works well for most applications.
  • Loose — More clearance; easier assembly, slightly more noise.
  • Custom — Enter an exact offset in mm. Positive values increase groove depth.
Use Standard unless you are tuning for a specific fit or 3D print tolerance issue.
Backlash
Controls the side-to-side play between belt teeth and pulley grooves.
  • None — Zero extra gap; maximum positional accuracy, risk of binding if belt is not perfectly aligned.
  • Tight — Minimal play; suitable for precision servo drives.
  • Standard — Safe default for most applications.
  • Loose — More play; reduces binding risk at the cost of positioning accuracy.
  • Custom — Enter an exact tooth-width offset in mm.
3D Print Compensation (mm)
Adds a uniform outward offset to all non-bore surfaces. Use this to compensate for filament over-extrusion or shrinkage on FDM printers. Typical range: 0.05 – 0.2 mm.
Set to 0 for laser-cut or machined parts. For FDM prints, start with 0.1 mm and iterate.

Download

Download Pulley SVG
Downloads a 2D SVG sketch of the pulley profile (teeth, bore, and outer diameter) ready to import into CAD tools such as Fusion 360, FreeCAD, Inkscape, or Illustrator. The geometry is drawn at a 1:1 mm scale.
In Fusion 360: Insert → Insert SVG → select the file → scale should be 1 mm/unit.
Download Belt
Downloads the belt in SVG or DXF format. SVG is best for CAD import. DXF exports the belt tooth cross-section profile in single-pulley mode, or the complete two-pulley belt layout with separate BELT_BACK and BELT_TEETH layers in Two Pulley Drive mode.
Download Pulley DXF
Downloads a DXF file of the pulley profile and bore using true arc entities. Output is in R2010 DXF format in millimetres, suitable for import into CAD applications that support DXF.
Download Pulley STL
Downloads a binary STL file of the complete 3D pulley solid, including any hub boss and retention features, ready for slicing or direct printing. Geometry is exported in millimetres.
Open in your slicer (PrusaSlicer, Cura, Bambu Studio, etc.) and print directly — no CAD import required.
Download Pulley STEP
Downloads a STEP (AP214) file of the complete 3D pulley solid as a proper B-rep solid, including hub boss, bore, set-screw holes, and captured nut pockets. Import directly into Fusion 360, SolidWorks, FreeCAD, or any CAD tool that accepts STEP files.
STEP is the best format for CAD editing — it imports as a solid body, not a mesh, so you can sketch on faces, add features, and modify dimensions.

3D — Hub

Enabling the Hub
The Hub section has a checkbox in its header. When checked, a cylindrical boss is built on top of the toothed section and included in the 3D preview and STL export. When unchecked, no hub geometry is generated — the pulley is a plain toothed ring with a through bore.
The Hub section is only visible when 3D Features is active.
Hub OD (mm)
The outer diameter of the hub boss. The hub may be narrower or wider than the pulley OD — there is no restriction. A hub narrower than the pulley sits recessed inside the tooth rim; a hub wider than the pulley creates a flange that overhangs the teeth.
Hub Height (mm)
The axial height of the hub boss above the top of the toothed section. The bore is subtracted through the full combined height (Belt Height + Hub Height) so the shaft hole runs uninterrupted from bottom to top of the finished part.
If a captured nut is selected and the hub height you enter is too short to fit the nut pocket, the height is raised automatically to the minimum required.
Retention Method — Set Screw (std.)
Drills one or two radial screw holes straight through the hub wall. The hole passes through the full hub diameter so the set screw can be driven from either side with a hex key.
  • 1 screw — single hole perpendicular to the shaft.
  • 2 screws — holes at 90° apart for a more secure grip.
Choose the screw size (M3 – M10) to set the hole diameter.
Retention Method — Set Screw (captured nut)
A stronger retention arrangement. Each screw is retained by a standard ISO hex nut that sits in a precision pocket in the hub boss.
  • Pocket geometry — The pocket opens from the top face of the hub. The nut is dropped in from above; two vertical flat faces of the nut locate against the pocket walls to prevent rotation. The nut sits against the shaft bore, and the screw enters from the hub OD, passes through the hub wall, threads into the nut, and its tip contacts the shaft.
  • Screw entry hole — A single-sided radial clearance hole is drilled from the hub OD inward to the nut pocket. There is no exit hole on the opposite wall.
  • Angular spacing — 2 screws are placed 180° apart (directly opposite).
  • Auto hub height — If the hub height is shorter than the nut requires, it is raised automatically.
  • Auto oblong lobe — If the hub OD does not provide at least two nut thicknesses of wall material outside the nut, the hub grows a rounded lobe in the screw direction to ensure adequate material.
Choose the screw size first — nut pocket dimensions are calculated automatically from the ISO standard for that size.
Captured nut retention is stronger than a plain set screw and can be retightened after printing without stripping the plastic bore wall.
Retention Method — D-Shaft
Adds a flat chord cut to the bore, matching a D-profile motor shaft. Enter the flat depth — the distance from the bore wall to the flat surface. Measure your actual shaft; a typical value is 0.5 mm for small shafts.

3D — Preview

3D Viewer
Click the 3D tab in the preview panel to open the interactive Three.js viewer. The pulley — including hub boss and all retention features — is rendered in real time and updates automatically as you change any setting.
  • Orbit — Left-click and drag to rotate the view.
  • Zoom — Scroll wheel to zoom in and out.
  • Pan — Right-click and drag (or two-finger drag on a trackpad) to pan.
The model is centred at the origin so it fits the view automatically after each update.
The preview geometry is exactly what the STL download produces — if it looks right in the viewer, the STL is correct.
