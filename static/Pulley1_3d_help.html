Hub

Enabling the Hub
Checkbox in the Hub section header. When checked, a cylindrical boss is built on top of the toothed section and included in the 3D preview and STL export. When unchecked, no hub geometry is generated.
The Hub section is only visible when 3D Features is active.
Hub OD (mm)
Outer diameter of the hub boss. May be narrower or wider than the pulley OD — no restriction. Narrower = hub boss recessed inside the tooth rim. Wider = hub flange overhanging the teeth.
Hub Height (mm)
Axial height of the hub boss above the toothed section. The bore runs through the full combined height (Belt Height + Hub Height). For captured nut retention, the height is auto-raised if the nut pocket requires more space than the entered value.

Shaft Retention

Retention — Set Screw (std.)
Radial screw hole(s) drilled through the full hub diameter — the screw can be driven from either side with a hex key. 1 screw = single perpendicular hole. 2 screws = holes placed 90° apart for more secure grip. Select screw size M3–M10 to set the hole diameter.
Retention — Set Screw (captured nut)
Custom solution for 3D printed parts, where metal nuts are used to hold set screws. A standard ISO hex nut is captured in a pocket that opens from the top face of the hub. The nut drops in from above; two vertical flat faces locate against the pocket walls to prevent rotation. The nut sits against the shaft bore. The screw enters from the hub OD, passes through the hub wall, threads into the nut, and contacts the shaft.

Screw entry is a one-sided radial clearance hole — no exit hole on the opposite wall. 2 screws are placed 180° apart (directly opposite).

Auto hub height: if the entered hub height is shorter than the nut requires, it is raised automatically. Auto oblong lobe: if the hub OD does not provide at least two nut-thicknesses of wall material outside the nut, the hub grows a rounded lobe in the screw direction.

Select screw size first — nut pocket dimensions are calculated automatically from the ISO standard for that size.
Captured nut retention is stronger than a plain set screw and can be retightened after printing without stripping the plastic bore wall.

Captured Nut Hub — 3D Printing Guide

Pocket Shape
The nut pocket has two zones:
___________ ← hub top face (open)
| |
| | ← rectangular upper section
| | (nut slides straight down)
\ /
\ / ← angled lower section
\ / (matches hex nut lower profile)
\ /
\ /
V ← pointed tip (seats hex nut vertex)
The upper rectangular section is the full flat-to-flat width of the nut plus 0.5 mm clearance on each side, so the nut slides in freely without binding. The pointed bottom cradles the nut's lower vertex, locating it axially so the screw hole and nut thread are perfectly aligned.
Assembly
1. Drop the hex nut into the pocket from the top — it can only enter in one orientation (flat sides facing the pocket walls).
2. The nut seats automatically at the bottom tip.
3. Thread the set screw through the side of the hub and into the nut.
4. Tighten against the shaft. The nut cannot rotate because two flat faces are held by the pocket walls.

The screw engages full metal threads in the nut — not printed plastic — so the joint can be tightened, loosened, and retightened many times without wear.
Use a drop of thread-locking compound (e.g. Loctite 243) on the set screw for vibration resistance without permanent bond.
Print Orientation
Print with the hub face up (toothed face on the bed). In this orientation:
• The pocket walls are vertical — no support needed inside the pocket.
• The V-shaped pocket bottom is self-supporting (bridging angle ≤ 45°).
• The screw hole is horizontal — prints cleanly with standard bridging.

If the hub must face down, add support inside the pocket. Remove support carefully before inserting the nut.
Do not print the pulley on its side. Tooth grooves printed perpendicular to the layer lines have poor surface finish and reduced strength.
Auto Hub Height
If the hub height entered is less than the tip-to-tip depth of the nut (the minimum pocket depth), the hub height is automatically increased so the pocket fits entirely within the hub boss. The updated height is used for the 3D model and STL export; the input field is not changed.
Auto Oblong Lobe
If the hub OD is too narrow to provide at least two nut-thicknesses of wall material between the nut and the hub outer surface, the hub footprint is automatically extended with a rounded lobe in the screw direction. The lobe adds only as much material as needed to meet the wall requirement — the rest of the hub stays circular. This is visible in the 3D preview.
To avoid the lobe, increase Hub OD until the warning disappears in the hub info line.
Recommended Print Settings
• Material: PETG or ASA for outdoor/hot environments; PLA+ for benchtop use.
• Infill: 40 % or higher around the hub — use gyroid or honeycomb for isotropic strength.
• Perimeters: 4–5 walls around the hub and bore area.
• Layer height: 0.15–0.2 mm for good pocket wall surface finish.
• Bore tolerance: print one test bore and measure before committing to a full batch.
Retention — D-Shaft
Adds a flat chord cut to the bore for a D-profile shaft. Enter the flat depth — distance from the bore wall to the flat. Measure your actual shaft; typical value 0.5 mm.

3D Preview

Viewer
Click the 3D tab in the preview panel to open the interactive Three.js viewer. The pulley — including hub boss and all retention features — renders in real time and updates automatically as settings change.

Orbit: left-click and drag.   Zoom: scroll wheel.   Pan: right-click and drag (or two-finger drag on trackpad).

Model is centred at origin and fits the view automatically.
The preview geometry is exactly what the STL download produces — if it looks right in the viewer, the STL is correct.
