Belt Profile

Family
HTD — high-torque drive, common in robotics and 3D printers.
GT / GT2 / GT3 — Gates tooth profile, precision motion control.
T-series (T2.5 / T5 / T10) — trapezoidal tooth, lower backlash at slow speeds.
MXL / XL / L / H — Imperial trapezoidal, US-market and legacy equipment.
Changing Family resets Pitch to only the available options for that family.
Pitch
Centre-to-centre distance between belt teeth in mm (e.g. 5M = 5 mm). Larger pitch = coarser drive, higher torque capacity. Smaller pitch = finer resolution, quieter operation.
Locked to Family — choose Family first, then Pitch.

Size

Input Number of Teeth
Determines pulley diameter directly — more teeth = larger pulley. A minimum tooth count is enforced per belt specification to prevent undercutting of the tooth profile.
In Two Pulley Drive mode, Pulley 1 has highest edit priority — changing its teeth keeps the Gear Ratio fixed and recalculates Pulley 2 automatically.
Auto Outer Diameter (mm)
Calculated from tooth count and pitch. Can also be typed directly — the tool finds the nearest whole-number tooth count that matches the entered diameter. In Two Pulley Drive mode, editing OD here also recalculates Pulley 2.
Input Bore Diameter (mm)
Central shaft hole diameter. Must be smaller than the pitch diameter. Minimum 1 mm.
For 3D-printed pulleys, add 0.2–0.4 mm over the nominal shaft diameter to account for FDM printing tolerance.
Read-only Pitch Diameter
Theoretical belt pitch-line diameter. Used for belt length calculations.
Formula: (Teeth × Pitch) / π

Advanced

Tooth Clearance
Extra room at the tip of each belt tooth in the groove. Tight = minimal clearance for precision fit. Standard = recommended default. Loose = easier assembly, slightly more noise. Custom = exact mm offset (positive = deeper groove).
Use Standard unless tuning for a specific belt fit or FDM dimensional tolerance.
Backlash
Side-to-side play between belt teeth and pulley grooves. None = zero gap, maximum accuracy, binding risk. Tight = minimal play, for precision servos. Standard = safe default. Loose = reduces binding risk. Custom = exact mm offset.
3D Print Compensation (mm)
Uniform outward offset applied to all non-bore surfaces. Compensates for FDM over-extrusion or material shrinkage. Typical range: 0.05–0.2 mm.
Set to 0 for laser-cut or machined parts. For FDM, start with 0.1 mm and iterate with a test print.
