STD / S-series — alternate names

These profiles appear under several names depending on the manufacturer or region. All refer to the same ISO 13050 S-series large-radius arc-flank geometry: S-series (ISO official), STD (Super Torque Drive), S-HTD, and Synchroflex S (ContiTech trade name). If a belt datasheet lists an S-series pitch code (e.g. S8M), it is compatible with this family regardless of the trade name used.

RPP vs STD — not the same profile

ISO 13050 defines two distinct families: STD/S-series uses a large-radius circular arc flank (Rbb); RPP uses a parabolic curve (y' = K·x'²) with a tip fillet. Check your belt datasheet — if the pitch code starts with R (e.g. R8M), select RPP; if it starts with S (e.g. S8M), select STD / S-series.

Size

  • Input Number of Teeth — Determines pulley diameter directly. A minimum tooth count is enforced per belt specification to prevent undercutting.
  • Auto Outer Diameter (mm) — Calculated from tooth count and pitch. Can also be typed — the tool finds the nearest whole-number tooth count.
  • Input Bore Diameter (mm) — Central shaft hole diameter. Must be smaller than the pitch diameter. Minimum 1 mm.
    For 3D-printed pulleys, add 0.2–0.4 mm over the nominal shaft diameter to account for FDM printing tolerance.
  • Input Bore Shape — the shape of the shaft hole, cut the same in every download, 2D and 3D:
    • Round — a plain bore.
    • D-flat — a flat chord for a D-profile motor shaft. Flat Depth is how far the flat sits in from the bore wall; measure your shaft (small motor shafts are typically 0.5 mm).
    • Keyway — a slot for a parallel key (ISO 773 / DIN 6885). Key Width W and Hub Depth (t2 + corner clearance r); ISO Key fills both from ISO 773 for your bore.
    • Spline — the hole for a splined shaft. Straight-sided (ISO 14): pick a size from the light or medium series, or type Splines N, Minor d, Major D and slot Width B. Involute (ISO 4156): Pressure angle 30°, 37.5° or 45°, Root (30° only), Module m and Teeth z. Hex bar: pick the Stock — metric, inch (1/4″–1″, FRC's 3/8″ and 1/2″) or rounded hex (REV's 1/2″, 13.75 mm across corners) — or type Across flats, the Rings that go on it (DIN 471, or inch SH / 5100) and, for rounded stock, Across corners. A flat faces +X. It's far easier to print than a spline and carries the torque on its own. A spline replaces the round bore: Bore Diameter becomes the hole's minor diameter and can't be typed until Bore Shape is Round again. Its major diameter is how far the bore reaches, for the wall checks and the Dimensions panel.
      • The fit is the standard's default, and the line under the sizes gives it: ISO 14's sliding fit (hole H9 / H10 / H7 on B / D / d, shaft d10 / a11 / f7, from ISO 286-2), or ISO 4156's H/h, tolerance class 6. A hex bar: an H11 hole on the h11 that EN 10278 gives bright hex bar (to 80 mm), across flats and across corners. The hole and a sample shaft that fits it are each drawn at the middle of their tolerance zone.
      • In 3D Mode, the Spline card (under Spokes) holds the rest. Retaining ring, top face / bottom face — either or both: a DIN 471 external ring (McMaster's 98541A…), the size for the shaft's outside diameter — on a hex bar, the round-shaft ring for its across flats (DIN 471 metric, SH / 5100 inch; SH-50 on 1/2″ hex): its groove runs round inside the flats, unbroken. One ring stops the pulley one way (a shoulder on the shaft takes the other); a ring on each face holds it both ways. Each ringed face gets a counterbore DIN 471's d4 wide (the room the ring's lugs need) and the groove's width deep, so the ring sits flush; the sample shaft carries a groove there. The depth is measured from the part's outer face: a flange or metal plate on that face (with no hub there) has the counterbore through it, and the pulley takes the rest. A separate printed top flange with that larger hole locates on its nubs rather than on the shaft.
      • Make counterbore — under each ticked ring, on by default. Untick it and that face stays plain: the washer lies on the face and the ring on the washer, so they stand proud by their thickness, and the sample shaft's groove and end move out with them. The part keeps the material a counterbore would take — worth it when the counterbore would leave too little round it (a flange over that face then keeps its own hole, too).
      • Splined washer under each ring — a thin washer with the same spline (d4 − 1 mm across, 1.5 mm thick) between the pulley and the ring: it turns with the shaft, so the ring rubs the washer, not the pulley. A counterbore gets deeper by its thickness.
      • Show sample shaft — the 3D view draws the sample shaft (grey) through the bore, with the rings (dark) and washers (blue) where they sit.
      A spline drives the pulley itself, so it takes no set screw: Retention (Hub, in 3D Mode) is None while Bore Shape is Spline. A hex bar takes any Retention — one or two set screws, each on a flat (+X, then 120° on; 180° with captured nuts) — with or without rings.
    Every feature sits on the +X side (0°): the flat, the key slot, the first spline slot. In 3D Mode a set screw (Hub → Retention) then goes on a flat or key — one screw.
    The involute's flanks are drawn as arcs within 0.001 mm of the true involute, and the SVG and DXF carry the spline as true lines and arcs. The STL adds the print compensation (Advanced): the bore and counterbore grow, the sample shaft and the washer's outside shrink, the shaft's groove widens. SVG, DXF and STEP are the nominal fit. The small_step STEP can't build a splined bore yet and says so.
    The Download window then lists the sample splined shaft (as long as the pulley, DIN 471's n past each ringed face and 5 mm past any other) and, with the box ticked, the splined washer — STL, SVG and DXF.
  • Read-only Pitch Diameter — Theoretical belt pitch-line diameter used for belt length calculations. Formula: (Teeth × Pitch) / π

Dimensions and Warnings

Under the preview, a collapsible Dimensions table lists the pulley's figures: pitch, outside and root diameters, groove depth, pitch line differential, belt and face width (with the minimum the belt's standard asks for), a spline's minor and major diameters with the sample shaft's, the retaining ring, its counterbore and the washer, and — with flanges in 3D Mode — the flange's outside diameter, how far it reaches past the OD, and the minimum flange height. A figure that falls short of its minimum is shown red; one with no published figure behind it is marked ≈ (hover it for why).

Above the table, warnings say where a published rule is broken, each naming its source:

  • Face width — the pulley narrower than ISO 13050 (HTD, GT 8/14M, STD 8/14M, RPP), ISO 5294 (MXL–XXH) or Gates (GT 2/3/5M) ask for the belt, flanged or unflanged. T, AT and STD 2/3/5M have no published figure here and aren't checked.
  • Flange height — the flange reaching less far past the OD than ISO 13050 Annex D (belt tooth height + pitch differential), ISO 5294 or Gates Table 22.
  • Flange angle — outside ISO's 8–25° (it's built at the nearest limit).
  • Spline reach — a spline's major diameter within 1 mm of the tooth root or the hub OD.
  • Ring counterbore — within 1 mm of the hub OD (on the top face), the spoke hub or the tooth root.

Auto-fix, beside the warnings, applies the settings that clear the ones a setting can: clearance height, flange rim radius, flange angle — and for a spline the part can't hold (1 mm of wall round its reach and the ring's counterbore), a bigger Hub OD, up to the tooth root less 1 mm a side; failing that, the largest smaller spline of the same kind (an ISO 14 size, or fewer involute teeth) that fits.

A new design's Clearance Height already makes the face the standard's minimum width, and follows the profile, belt width and flanges until you type a value of your own.

Download — 2D

  • SVG Drawing — Clean 2D SVG at 1:1 mm scale (teeth, bore, OD). Import into Fusion 360 (Insert → Insert SVG, scale 1 mm/unit), FreeCAD, Inkscape, or Illustrator. This is also what the 2D preview window shows.
  • SVG + Data — Same drawing plus a data panel listing all key dimensions. Useful for documentation and inspection records.
  • Download Pulley DXF — DXF of the pulley profile and bore using true arc entities. R2010 DXF format, dimensions in millimetres.
  • Download Belt (SVG or DXF) — In single-pulley mode: belt tooth cross-section only. In Two Pulley Drive mode: the complete two-pulley belt layout with separate BELT_BACK and BELT_TEETH layers.

Import Design

The 📂 Import button (toolbar, next to Reset) restores a previously exported design. Every SVG, DXF, STEP, and STL file downloaded from this tool contains embedded design parameters. Clicking Import, then selecting one of those files, reads the parameters and repopulates all form fields exactly as they were when the file was exported.

Only files exported from this tool contain embedded parameters. Files from other CAD programs will show an error.
