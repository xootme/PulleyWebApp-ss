Hub

Enabling the Hub
Checkbox in the Hub section header. When checked, a cylindrical boss is built on top of Pulley 2's toothed section. Pulley 2's hub settings are completely independent of Pulley 1's — each pulley can have a different hub OD, height, and retention method.
Hub OD (mm)
Outer diameter of the hub boss. May be narrower or wider than the pulley OD. Narrower tucks the boss inside the tooth rim; wider creates a flange overhanging the teeth.
Hub Height (mm)
Axial height above the toothed section. The bore runs through the full combined height (Belt Height + Hub Height). For captured nut retention, the height is auto-raised if the nut pocket requires more space than the entered value.
Retention — Set Screw (std.)
Radial screw hole(s) through the full hub diameter. 2 screws placed 90° apart. Choose screw size M3–M10 to set the hole diameter.
Retention — Set Screw (captured nut)
ISO hex nut captured in a top-opening pocket in the hub boss. Nut drops in from the hub top face; two vertical flat faces locate against pocket walls to prevent rotation. Screw enters from the hub OD and threads into the nut against the shaft bore. One-sided radial entry hole — no exit hole on the opposite wall. 2 screws placed 180° apart.

Hub height is auto-raised if too short; a rounded lobe is added to the hub OD if there is insufficient wall material (at least 2 nut-thicknesses of hub required outside the nut). Select screw size M3–M10; pocket is sized automatically from the ISO standard.
Captured nut retention survives multiple tighten/loosen cycles without stripping a printed hub.

Captured Nut Hub — 3D Printing Guide

Pocket Shape
The nut pocket has two zones:
___________ ← hub top face (open)
| |
| | ← rectangular upper section
| | (nut slides straight down)
\ /
\ / ← angled lower section
\ / (matches hex nut lower profile)
\ /
\ /
V ← pointed tip (seats hex nut vertex)
The upper rectangular section is the full flat-to-flat width of the nut plus 0.5 mm clearance on each side, so the nut slides in freely without binding. The pointed bottom cradles the nut's lower vertex, locating it axially so the screw hole and nut thread are perfectly aligned.
Assembly
1. Drop the hex nut into the pocket from the top — it can only enter in one orientation (flat sides facing the pocket walls).
2. The nut seats automatically at the bottom tip.
3. Thread the set screw through the side of the hub and into the nut.
4. Tighten against the shaft. The nut cannot rotate because two flat faces are held by the pocket walls.

The screw engages full metal threads in the nut — not printed plastic — so the joint can be tightened, loosened, and retightened many times without wear.
Use a drop of thread-locking compound (e.g. Loctite 243) on the set screw for vibration resistance without permanent bond.
Print Orientation
Print with the hub face up (toothed face on the bed). In this orientation:
• The pocket walls are vertical — no support needed inside the pocket.
• The V-shaped pocket bottom is self-supporting (bridging angle ≤ 45°).
• The screw hole is horizontal — prints cleanly with standard bridging.

If the hub must face down, add support inside the pocket. Remove support carefully before inserting the nut.
Do not print the pulley on its side. Tooth grooves printed perpendicular to the layer lines have poor surface finish and reduced strength.
Auto Hub Height
If the hub height entered is less than the tip-to-tip depth of the nut (the minimum pocket depth), the hub height is automatically increased so the pocket fits entirely within the hub boss. The updated height is used for the 3D model and STL export; the input field is not changed.
Auto Oblong Lobe
If the hub OD is too narrow to provide at least two nut-thicknesses of wall material between the nut and the hub outer surface, the hub footprint is automatically extended with a rounded lobe in the screw direction. The lobe adds only as much material as needed to meet the wall requirement — the rest of the hub stays circular. This is visible in the 3D preview.
To avoid the lobe, increase Hub OD until the warning disappears in the hub info line.
Recommended Print Settings
• Material: PETG or ASA for outdoor/hot environments; PLA+ for benchtop use.
• Infill: 40 % or higher around the hub — use gyroid or honeycomb for isotropic strength.
• Perimeters: 4–5 walls around the hub and bore area.
• Layer height: 0.15–0.2 mm for good pocket wall surface finish.
• Bore tolerance: print one test bore and measure before committing to a full batch.
Retention — D-Shaft
Flat chord cut to the bore for a D-profile shaft. Enter flat depth in mm; typical value 0.5 mm — measure your actual shaft.
