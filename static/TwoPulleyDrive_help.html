Overview

Enable Two Pulley Drive to design a complete belt drive system with two pulleys. The tool calculates the required belt length, validates the centre distance, and keeps the gear ratio consistent as you adjust teeth counts.
Gear Ratio R = TeethP2 TeethP1
Belt Length L ≈ 2C + π2 (D1 + D2) + (D2 − D1)2 4C

Two Pulley Drive Toggle

Two Pulley Drive (checkbox)
When checked, the Pulley 2 panel appears on the right, and the spacing and ratio controls become active in the drive bar. When unchecked, only Pulley 1 is shown and all dual-drive controls are hidden.
Settings for both pulleys are saved automatically — toggling off and back on restores your previous values.

Spacing Controls

Centre Distance (mm) Input
The distance between the centres of Pulley 1 and Pulley 2. This is the primary layout dimension of your belt drive. Changing this value automatically recalculates the required belt tooth count.
After you stop typing, the centre distance snaps to the nearest value achievable by a whole-number belt tooth count (alignment to pitch).
If the entered distance is less than the minimum required (shown in red), the pulleys would overlap — increase the centre distance or reduce pulley sizes.
Minimum Distance Note
Displayed in red when the centre distance is too small for the selected pulley sizes. The minimum is approximately: Cmin ≈ OD1 + OD2 2 + clearance
Belt Teeth Auto
The number of teeth on the belt, calculated from the centre distance and pulley diameters using the open belt length formula. You can also type a specific belt tooth count and the centre distance will update to match.
Standard belt lengths are sold in fixed tooth counts. Use this field to match an off-the-shelf belt and find the resulting centre distance.

Ratio Controls

Gear Ratio Auto
The speed reduction (or increase) provided by the drive:
  • Ratio = 1.0 — Both pulleys same speed (same tooth count).
  • Ratio > 1.0 — Pulley 2 spins slower than Pulley 1 (torque multiplication).
  • Ratio < 1.0 — Pulley 2 spins faster than Pulley 1 (speed increase).
R = TeethP2 TeethP1
After you stop typing, the ratio snaps to the true ratio achievable with whole-number tooth counts.
▲ / ▼ Arrow Buttons
Increments or decrements the Pulley 2 tooth count by one tooth, providing fine control over the gear ratio. Use these to step through ratio options while keeping Pulley 1 fixed.
Ratio Note (e.g. "20T : 30T")
Shows the actual tooth counts for Pulley 1 and Pulley 2 that produce the displayed ratio. This is the ground truth — the ratio field rounds for display but the tooth counts are exact.

Reset Button

Reset
Clears all saved settings from browser storage and reloads the page with factory defaults. Use this if inputs get into an inconsistent state or you want to start fresh.
All customisations (both pulleys, centre distance, ratio) will be lost. This cannot be undone.

Workflow Tips

Designing for a known belt: Set your centre distance first, note the calculated Belt Teeth, then match to the nearest standard belt length by adjusting Belt Teeth and reading back the corrected centre distance.
Designing for a target ratio: Set Pulley 1 teeth, enter your desired ratio, and the Pulley 2 teeth will update. Then set your centre distance. Fine-tune with the ▲ / ▼ buttons.
Downloading: Each pulley has its own Download SVG and Download Pulley DXF button in its panel. For the complete belt drive, use the Download Belt button in the Pulley 1 panel — it offers both SVG and DXF formats. SVG is best for CAD import; DXF exports the full belt layout with separate BELT_BACK and BELT_TEETH layers.
