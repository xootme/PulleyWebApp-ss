Print Settings for Best Results

SettingRecommendation
Print orientationFlat in the XY plane — pulley face down on the bed. Tooth flanks printed in-plane are far stronger than teeth built up in layers.
Wall count4–6 perimeters minimum. Increase walls until the slicer fills the tooth cross-section completely.
Infill40–60 % minimum; gyroid or honeycomb for isotropic strength.
Layer height0.15–0.20 mm for better surface detail on tooth flanks. Coarser layers leave stair-step ridges.
MaterialPETG — good starting point, low warp.
ABS / ASA — stiffer, better under sustained load.
Nylon (PA12, PA-CF) — best toughness for high-torque drives.
Avoid PLA for anything beyond light duty.
Print Extra(From 2D pulley design page) Start at 0.1 mm and test-fit the belt. A snug but free-running fit maximises tooth engagement.
Tooth countMore teeth in mesh = load shared across more teeth. Prefer larger-diameter pulleys on the high-torque shaft where space allows.
Quick rule of thumb: if a printed pulley strips teeth repeatedly, first increase wall count to fully solid teeth, then upgrade material before increasing tooth count or belt width.
Resin (SLA/MSLA) prints are dimensionally accurate but brittle — not recommended for high-torque drives. Set Print Extra to 0 for resin.

Shaft Retention — Captured Nut Hub

The captured nut option is a custom solution for 3D printed parts where a standard hex nut replaces a tapped hole to hold the set screw. This is stronger than threading directly into plastic and survives multiple tighten/loosen cycles without stripping.
How it works
A pocket opens from the top face of the hub. The pocket has a rectangular upper section that guides the nut in, and a V-shaped lower section that matches the hex nut's lower tip and seats it axially. The set screw enters radially from the hub OD, passes through the hub wall, threads into the nut, and its tip contacts the shaft.
Nut dimensions are taken from ISO 4032:1999 — Hexagon nuts, style 1, product grades A and B.
Assembly
  1. Print the pulley with hub face up (hub on top).
  2. Insert the hex nut into the pocket from above — it should seat snugly with no play. It may also help to add a drop of cyanoacrylate glue to secure the nut in the hole.
  3. Thread the set screw in from the hub OD until it contacts the shaft.
  4. Tighten to clamp the shaft. The nut is captured and cannot rotate.
Print the hub with extra walls (6+) around the nut pocket. Thin walls between the pocket and hub OD are the first failure point under high torque.
Auto Hub Height & Oblong Lobe
If your entered hub height is shorter than the nut requires, it is raised automatically to the minimum. If the hub OD does not provide enough wall material outside the nut, the hub grows a rounded lobe in the screw direction — this is normal and intentional.

Bore & Shaft Fit

Dimensional tolerance
FDM printers typically print bores 0.2–0.4 mm undersize due to material expansion. Enter the nominal shaft diameter and add 0.2–0.4 mm to the bore field as a starting point, then test-fit and adjust.
D-Shaft
For D-profile shafts, enable the D-Shaft retention option and enter the flat depth — the distance from the bore wall to the flat face of the shaft. Measure your shaft directly; a typical value is 0.5 mm for small motor shafts.
