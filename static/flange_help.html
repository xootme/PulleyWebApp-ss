Flanges

Flanges are annular plates fitted to the faces of a pulley to prevent the belt from riding off axially. Check the checkbox next to Flanges to enable them. Each pulley has its own independent flange configuration.

Flange Type

Select the type with the 3D Print checkbox:

TypeDescriptionBest for
Metal (default) Thin flat plate that bends upward at the outer rim. Matches stamped or laser-cut sheet metal. One plate on each face of the pulley. Metal pulleys; standard industrial flanges
3D Print Solid wedge cross-section. The bottom flange is permanently merged into the pulley body. The top flange can optionally be a separate part for post-assembly gluing. Plastic pulleys; custom print-in-place designs

Inner Rim Rule

How far each flange extends inward depends on the flange type:

Metal flanges & 3D-print top flange

  • Spokes active — inner face of the solid rim ring = tooth OD radius − Rim Depth. The flange covers only the rim ring; it does not span the spoke web or hub boss area.
  • No spokes, hub present — hub OD.
  • No spokes, no hub — bore diameter.

3D-print bottom flange

  • Spokes active — inner face of the solid rim ring (same as above).
  • No spokes — always extends to the bore, regardless of hub settings. Because the bottom flange is merged into the pulley body, a solid disc from flange OD to bore is structurally correct and avoids a gap around the hub boss.

Shared Settings

  • Flange Angle — Angle of the angled section from horizontal (8–25°). Steeper angles provide better belt retention; shallower angles are easier to assemble. 15° matches most commercial flanges.
  • Rim Radius — Horizontal distance the angled section extends beyond the tooth OD (mm). Typically 2–5 mm.

Both are marked on the shape pictures below — for metal and for 3D-printed flanges.

Metal Flange Settings

  • Plate Thickness — Material stock thickness (mm). The outer rim of the plate is bent upward to create the flange angle. Typical: 0.5–1.0 mm thin sheet; 1.0–3.0 mm heavier plate.
  • Bend Radius — Horizontal distance from the tooth OD to the end of the bend arc (mm). Set to 0 for automatic (1.5 × plate thickness). A tighter bend is stiffer; too tight can crack thin sheet metal.

Metal flanges are two plates, one on each face; they come in the pulley's STL download, already placed on its faces.

3D Print Flange Settings

  • Flange Height — Thickness of the flange where it leaves the teeth (mm). The angled face then rises toward the rim, so the outer lip is thinner: lip = Flange Height − Rim Radius × tan(Flange Angle). Typical: 1.0–2.5 mm.
  • Top Flange as Separate Part (default: on) — When checked, the top flange is exported as a separate STL and shown above the pulley in the 3D preview. When unchecked, the top flange is merged into the pulley body as one combined solid.

Print Supports (Integrated Top Flange Only)

When the top flange is integrated (not separate), a breakaway support can be added under the top flange's overhang — the part of the flange outside the teeth.

  • Add Print Supports — Enables the support structure. A thin-walled tube sits 1 mm outside the lower flange edge; radial ribs inside it stand on the lower flange and stop just under the upper flange's overhang (the Air Gap). Two 1 mm slits cut through opposite sides of the tube for easy removal after printing. Supports are only shown in the export file (Assembly STL) — not in the 3D preview.
  • Nozzle Dia. (mm) — Nozzle diameter of your printer (default 0.4 mm). Controls rib tip width and tube wall thickness so the supports are one extrusion width wide.
  • Max Spacing (mm) — Maximum angular spacing between adjacent support ribs (default 10 mm). Smaller values add more ribs for better overhang support; rib count is always a multiple of 4.
  • Air Gap (mm) — Vertical gap between the support rib tips and the flange underside (default 0.2 mm). A small gap ensures the supports break away cleanly. Set to 0 for maximum contact (harder to remove).
Support ribs are included in the Assembly STL download. The pulley STL and the standalone flange STL do not include the support structure — print the assembly file when supports are needed.

Gluing Nubs (3D Print, separate top flange only)

Nubs are small cylindrical pins on the underside of the top flange that seat into matching sockets in the pulley face, centering the flange and increasing bonding strength.

  • Number of Nubs — Equally spaced around the nub centre circle. If adjacent nubs overlap they are merged automatically.
  • Nub Diameter — Diameter of each cylindrical pin and its matching socket (mm).
  • Nub Height — Depth of the socket cut into the pulley face (mm). The physical pin on the flange is shorter by the Socket Fit Allowance, leaving a small gap at the socket base when fully seated. Min: 1 mm. Max: one-third of belt height.
  • Socket Fit Allowance — Clearance subtracted from both the nub diameter and nub height (but not from the socket dimensions), so the pin slides freely into the socket. Default 0.2 mm. Increase for looser fit; decrease for a tighter press fit.
Nub centres lie on a circle at radius = groove-bottom radius − min(tooth height, 3 mm) − nub radius. If a nub extends inward past the inner rim boundary (spoke or hub), it is clipped at that boundary.

Hub and Flange

When the hub is wider than the pulley itself — a large Hub OD, or a captured-nut hub whose lobes reach past the teeth — it would overlap the top flange. The hub then starts on top of the flange instead, so the flange stays whole and the hub sits on it.

Downloading Flanges

Flanges come with the pulley in the Download window:

  • 3D Print, top flange as a separate part — the top flange is its own STL, with its nub pins if nubs are on; the bottom flange is part of the pulley's file.
  • 3D Print, top flange merged, with supports — the Assembly STL: pulley, both flanges and the support tube and ribs in one file. Print this one.
  • 3D Print, top flange merged, no supports — both flanges are part of the pulley's file.
  • Metal — both plates are in the pulley's STL, placed on its faces.
