⚙ Spokes — 2D Guide

Enabling Spokes hollows out the pulley body between the outer rim ring and the hub boss, replacing solid material with evenly spaced radial spokes. The cutout profile appears in the 2D SVG and DXF exports and in the 3D preview.

Spoke Count

Number of equally spaced radial spokes. Range: 2–24. Even counts (4, 6, 8) are typical for symmetry. Higher counts on large pulleys fill more of the web area.

Hub OD (mm)

Outer diameter of the central hub boss that the spokes attach to at their inner end. This sets where the spoke root starts. Must be larger than the bore diameter.

Click the picture to open it full size.

When spokes are active, Hub OD in the 3D panel is locked to this value — the 3D hub boss matches the spoke hub OD automatically.

Rim Depth (mm)

Radial thickness of the solid outer rim ring, measured inward from the bottom of the tooth grooves. The rim ring is the structural band that carries the tooth loads; the spokes attach to its inner face.

Click the picture to open it full size.

Spoke Width (mm)

Width of each spoke, measured across it. The spoke's sides are parallel, so it is this width all the way from the hub to the rim; only the fillets flare it wider where it meets the rim (Fillet Tip) and the hub (Fillet Base).

Click the picture to open it full size.

Fillet Tip (mm)

Corner radius where the spoke meets the inner face of the rim ring. A larger fillet tip reduces stress concentration at the rim junction and improves fatigue life. Set to 0 for a sharp corner.

If adjacent fillet arcs would overlap (spoke count is high and fillet tip is large), the settings no longer fit — see When the settings don't fit below.

Fillet Base (mm)

Corner radius where the spoke meets the outer face of the hub boss. A larger fillet base is particularly important for load-bearing spokes — it distributes bending stress over a wider area.

Click the picture to open it full size.

Fillet Base and Fillet Tip must leave a straight section of spoke wall between them. If they don't, the spoke settings no longer fit — see When the settings don't fit below.

When the settings don't fit

Spoke settings are in millimetres, so settings that suit a large pulley can be impossible on a smaller one — after choosing a smaller pitch or fewer teeth, for example: the fillets collide, the spokes crowd the rim, or the hub fills the space. When that happens a red warning appears under the spoke settings, listing the values that were changed so the spokes fit, such as Fillet Base: 3 → 1.25 mm.

  • The preview and every download already use those fitted values, so nothing fails to build. The numbers you typed stay in the fields.
  • Click Auto-fit to put the fitted values into the fields. Or change the settings yourself; the warning goes away as soon as they fit.
  • Changes are made in the least disruptive order: first room between hub and rim (Hub OD and Rim Depth shrink together, keeping at least a 1 mm rim and a 1 mm hub wall), then Fillet Base, Fillet Tip, Spoke Width, and last Spoke Count.
  • If no spokes fit at all — a very small pulley, or a bore that fills it — the warning says so and the spokes are left out. Turn Spokes off, or use a smaller bore or bigger pulley.

Spoke Height (mm) (3D only)

Thickness of the spoke web, centred in the pulley's height. The hub and the rim stay full height; only the spokes get thinner.

  • 0 = full height (the spokes are as thick as the pulley).
  • A positive value less than the pulley's height leaves a pocket above and below each spoke, each (pulley height − Spoke Height) ÷ 2 deep — lighter, while the rim that carries the teeth and the hub on the shaft keep their full height.

Click the picture to open it full size.

Spoke Height is only visible in 3D Mode. The 2D SVG/DXF profile always shows the full spoke cutout regardless of this setting.
Partial-height spoke webs (Spoke Height > 0) export to STEP with their fillets intact, and nub sockets are cut and fused into the pocket at any nub depth (no ribbon). This works for both narrow spokes and very wide spokes on a small hub (where the base fillets don't reach the hub) — the recessed web is built as a connected hub collar with radial spokes.

2D Export Behaviour

When spokes are enabled, the 2D SVG and DXF downloads include the spoke void cutout profiles. These can be used directly in laser or waterjet cutting software — the outer tooth profile, the bore, and the spoke voids are all separate closed paths on the same drawing.

Use Gen. Rim Layer to download the toothed rim ring as a separate 2D file — this is the outer portion of the pulley without the spoke web, suitable for layer-cake stacking methods. The checkbox is under each pulley's Download button, and only appears in 2D mode when that pulley's Spokes box is ticked. The rim-layer file also holds a hub washer (the ring between Hub OD and the bore) to stack in the middle as a spacer.
